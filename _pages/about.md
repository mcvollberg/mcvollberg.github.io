I study the thoughts and feelings behind how people treat one another — why we empathize, help, and cooperate, and why those tendencies have predictable limits. I lead an independent research group at the Zurich Center for Neuroeconomics (University of Zurich), supported by an SNSF Ambizione grant, in collaboration with the Swiss Center for Affective Sciences (University of Geneva). I completed my PhD in Cognition, Brain, & Behavior at Harvard University (committee: Mina Cikara, Daniel Schacter, and Elizabeth Phelps), followed by an SNSF postdoctoral fellowship. 

My work draws on behavioral experiments, neurophysiological measurement, and computational approaches at the intersection of affective science, behavioral economics, cognitive science, and social psychology. It has appeared in journals including Cognition, Emotion, JEP:General, Nature Human Behaviour, and Trends in Cognitive Sciences. I have been recognized with early career awards from the Swiss Psychological Society and the European Society for Cognitive Psychology, as well as multiple teaching awards from Harvard.
